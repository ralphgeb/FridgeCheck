Halo privacy policy

Last updated 17 November 2026

Halo is a personal health tracker for iPhone and Android. It reads your health data, with read-only access, from the source you connect: Fitbit through the Google Health API, Apple Health (iPhone and Apple Watch), Whoop, or Health Connect on Android. It works out its scores on your phone.

What Halo reads

Sleep and naps, heart rate, heart rate variability, resting heart rate, recovery, workouts, steps and activity, calories, breathing rate, blood oxygen, skin temperature, VO₂ max and weight, only as allowed by the permissions you grant. Halo never writes to, changes or deletes anything in any of these services.

Where your data goes

Your health data stays on your phone. Halo has no servers that store it, no accounts, no analytics and no advertising. It never sells, shares or transfers your data to anyone, and never uses it for advertising or to train any AI or machine-learning model.

Google user data

What Halo accesses. When you connect Fitbit, Halo uses Google sign-in to read, read-only, from the Google Health API: activity and fitness (steps, active calories, heart-rate zones, Active Zone Minutes, exercise sessions and heart rate), health metrics and measurements (resting heart rate, heart rate variability, blood oxygen, breathing rate, skin temperature, VO₂ max and weight), and sleep (sleep sessions, stages and naps). If you choose “Show your band's battery”, it also reads the list of your paired Fitbit devices, only to show your band's battery level. Halo never writes, changes or deletes anything in your Google account.

How it's used. Only to show you your own scores, graphs, insights and widgets in the app. Halo doesn't use it for advertising, for credit or lending decisions, or for anything other than the features you see. It isn't used to develop, improve or train any AI or machine-learning model, and it isn't sent to any AI service.

Sharing and transfer. Halo doesn't sell, share or transfer Google user data, raw or aggregated, to anyone. It goes straight from Google to your phone over an encrypted connection (HTTPS). There's no Halo server, no Halo account, no analytics and no advertising SDK, so the developer never receives it.

Protection. Google user data is stored only on your phone, in Halo's own private storage, which iOS and Android encrypt and keep from other apps. Google sign-in tokens are kept in the iPhone's Keychain, or on Android in Halo's private app storage. No one but you, including the developer, has access to it.

Retention and deletion. Halo keeps up to the last year of days on your phone and drops older days as new ones come in. Disconnecting Fitbit in Halo's Settings signs you out and revokes Halo's access with Google. Settings › Delete all data erases everything Halo has stored, and deleting the app removes it all. You can also remove Halo's access at any time at myaccount.google.com › Third-party apps & services.

Halo's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements, and to the Google Health API Developer and User Data Policy.

Sign-in

Sign-in tokens are kept in your iPhone's Keychain, or on Android in Halo's private app storage. For Whoop, sign-in passes through a small service that swaps a sign-in code for an access token and stores nothing. Google sign-in goes directly between your phone and Google.

Halo Plus

Subscriptions are bought and managed by Apple through the App Store. Halo only learns whether your subscription is active; it never sees your payment details.

Removing your data

Disconnect a source in Halo's Settings, use Settings › Delete all data, revoke access in your Google account, Apple Health, Health Connect or Whoop, or delete the app to remove everything it stored. Because Halo keeps nothing on any server, there is nothing else to delete.

Children

Halo is not directed at children under 13.
